## Findings (computed from the tables above)

1. Our frame-level peaks differ from Beat This!'s official output by at most 0.00 ms on generated audio: no frame-centring / hop / window bug in our use of the model.
2. Parabolic sub-frame interpolation moved beats by +4.80, -2.08, +1.35 ms relative to integer frames on the three generated tracks, i.e. away from Beat This!'s own output. It was a real (small) bias in our code and is now off by default (`PEAK_SUBFRAME_INTERPOLATION`).
3. Resampling: the same render at 5 rates gives offsets spanning 0.00 ms -- no resampling delay.
4. Decoders: WAV/FLAC/MP3/M4A offsets span 3.30 ms (lossy encoder priming, mostly compensated by ffmpeg).
5. On the substitutes the human annotations sit late of the audio's own attacks (sub-disco +11.2; sub-hiphop +21.8; sub-rock +2.6; sub-jazz +3.8; sub-country +28.5 ms), while the tracker sits closer to them (sub-disco +2.7; sub-hiphop +4.9; sub-rock +6.4; sub-jazz +3.1; sub-country +26.9 ms); Beat This!'s official output shows the same offset to the annotations as ours. The 'early' grid on GTZAN is mostly the annotation convention (tapped beats land after the attack), not a pipeline bug. No offset was added to compensate.
6. What remains is per-track placement of a few ms by the model itself (E1) and the dependence on where an onset falls inside a 20 ms frame (E2); the line fit averages the latter over many beats.
